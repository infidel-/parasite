# Textures & image generation (3D art)

The gpt-image → `textures-src/` → `make tex` → `app/textures/` pipeline. Loaded on demand; the
`3d-art` skill triggers it. `CLAUDE.md` keeps only the two rules that fire unprompted ("new images"
means generate them, and never stretch a texture).

**New findings go here, not into `CLAUDE.md`.** One bullet per lesson, the number that proves it
inline. When a lesson invalidates an old bullet, replace it rather than appending a second.

See also [`3d-models.md`](3d-models.md) — a generated prop needs a reference image from this
pipeline, and an emitted box needs a tiling texture from it. And
[`3d-geometry.md`](3d-geometry.md) for what an emitted thing asks of its art: **one texture per
FACE**, when a face maps 0..1 instead of tiling and the aspect lock that costs, and why hue is fixed
by re-rolling and never by `lift`.

## The rules

- **"we need new images" / "new images" means GENERATE them with the gpt-image MCP tool** (not "the user will supply them"). Treat it as an instruction to run `generate_image`/`edit_image` yourself. Decals now come out of gpt already cut (see the transparent-background bullet); hand-cutting is only for what that route fumbles.
- **Default to 1k — do NOT ask.** Two MCP servers: `gpt-image-2` (1024 edge) and `gpt-image-2-2k` (up to 3840 edge). Use `gpt-image-2` unless the user explicitly asks for 2k. It is the right answer nearly always: outputs bake down to 512 (details/sprites 256), so a 1024 source is already oversampled. Reach for 2k only on the user's say-so.
- **And default to `quality: "medium"` — do NOT pass `"high"` unless asked.** At 1k the tiers are **low $0.006 / medium $0.053 / high $0.211** per image, so `high` is **4x** medium for art that is downsampled to 512 before it is ever drawn. `medium` is the tool's own default; every sheet quoted in this file at ~$0.21 was a `high` call and did not need to be. The tier is not part of the packed-vs-solo trade below — it multiplies both sides of it.
- **FOUR 512 textures fit in ONE 1k generation, at a quarter of the cost — and WHICH ROUTE IS THE USER'S CALL, SO ASK.** Whenever a job wants two or more square tiles at once, say what each route buys and costs and ask which one, BEFORE generating anything. It is not a pure saving: the neighbours change the art, and that cuts both ways. Do not default to either.

  `"crop": [x, y, w, h]` in `textures.json` takes a sub-rect of the source, so several labels share one generated sheet; the crop runs BEFORE the lift, so each tile still takes its own gamma. A 2x2 of 1024 gives four native 512s, which is what the runtime ships anyway. Measured on the four square `facility/deco-*` tiles: **$0.2137 for the sheet against 4x that** — both figures at `quality: "high"`, which is itself 4x medium and was not needed (see the tier bullet above) — and on screen the bookcase differed from its four solo sources by mean |d| **0.5 / 255**. Two things it costs, and one it buys:
  - **wrap error roughly doubles** — a quadrant was composed to butt against its neighbours, not against itself. Measured wrapH/wrapV **3.4-4.5 / 3.8-9.9** against the solo sources' **1.2-2.5 / 2.0-4.3**. Invisible on a fitting that shows one or two repeats; do NOT pack a texture that tiles across a whole floor or wall.
  - **A PROMPTED "SEAMLESS TILEABLE" SWATCH IS OFTEN A COMPOSITION, AND WRAP ERROR CANNOT SEE IT.** `facility/deco-machine-*` asked for a repeating machine rhythm three times over and got PLACED features every time — a control strip of buttons and two dials along the bottom edge, a bolt column down each side, two grille patches and a cable gland on the lid. Its wrap measured **2.9-4.9 / 2.6-3.0**, *better* than the packed norm above, because wrap only tests whether the EDGES join; the interior can be a picture and the number never moves. Tiled off a world coordinate it shipped as misaligned fronts and lids with their corners cut off. **Open the drop and ask what is PLACED in it.** Something with placed features must map 0..1 over its whole face — which locks the face's aspect to the crop's, so a square swatch makes a cubic object. That is a real cost and it is cheaper than the alternative.
  - **a packed sheet does not have to be a 2x2 of squares, and that LIFTS THE ASPECT FLOOR.** A standalone 1k gen cannot go under about **0.64** because it must clear 655,360 px with no edge over 1024 — a CROP has no such floor. `deco-bookcase-front` is a 616x1024 sub-rect at aspect **0.6016**, narrower than any solo gen could be, with the family's two tiling swatches in the strip beside it. The one thing a sheet still cannot serve is an alpha cut: `background:"transparent"` cuts the WHOLE image.
  - **the model does NOT honour a stated split — measure the seams off the drop.** Asked for a vertical line at three quarters, it drew one at **616**; the horizontal one it did put at 512. A per-column mean `|diff|` peaks hard on a real seam (**45.5** against a background of ~4), so find them, then derive the crops and any aspect-locked constant from what it actually drew. Inset a tiling crop from the seams and keep it SQUARE — a swatch resized off its own aspect is a stretch.
  - **the neighbours fix the chroma the prose could not.** Cardboard beside grey steel in one frame came back at channel spread **48**, warm kraft at last, after three solo rolls that landed 67 / 8.5 / 58.6 and shipped grey. The model differentiates what it draws side by side — which is the argument FOR packing a family, and the argument against packing four unrelated tiles that should not have been harmonised.
  - **No wall-clock saving** — four `generate_image` calls in one message already run in parallel. The saving is money, not time.
  - Solve each tile's `lift` so the packed tile lands on the SAME built mean as the roll it replaces; then an A/B in game compares the ART and not the exposure. Park the superseded solo sources in `textures-src/unused/` — `make tex` skips that shelf, and the swap stays one edit from being undone.
- **One flat art style, from `style-flat.png`:** moody-night roguelike, matte flat colors, very dark/muted/desaturated palette, low contrast, even flat lighting, subtle fine grain, simple clean shapes, minimal detail. NO photorealism, NO realistic/volumetric lighting, NO glossy/specular reflections, NO camera/lens effects, NO soft photographic shadows — hand-painted, flat, like a 2D game tile. Append a canonical style string (top-level key in `art/textures.json`) VERBATIM to EVERY gen/edit prompt; keep it in sync with this paragraph. **There are TWO of them and the choice is what the image IS**: `style` for a SURFACE — a tiling texture, a decal, a sprite sheet, everything on this page — and `style_prop` for an OBJECT, i.e. a TRELLIS reference image ([`3d-models.md`](3d-models.md)). They are the same string but for the palette clause: "very dark and muted desaturated palette, low contrast" is correct for a wall, because a wall really is grey, and is wrong for a manufactured object, because its colour is a fact about it. Measured before the split: 203 shipped textures at median channel spread **7.8**, and 9 of 38 props shipped neutral.
- **Alpha comes from the API now: `background:"transparent"` (gpt-image-2 preview, on both MCP servers).** Send it with `output_format:"png"` (webp also carries alpha; **jpeg is rejected** — HTTP 400 `invalid_transparent_background_output_format`) and the drop has a real alpha channel. Measured on a test prop: 71.7% of pixels at alpha 0 and a 1–2px edge ramp — a cleaner cut than the `tol:24` chroma key. Two things ride along:
  - **Say NOTHING about a background in the prompt.** Describe one and the model paints one anyway. Still say the subject must not touch the frame edges.
  - Opaque pixels land at alpha **253, never 255**. Harmless here (`alphaTest` is 0.35–0.5 everywhere, `has_alpha` wants `lo < 255`) — just never write an `alpha == 255` test.

  Register the drop `class:"sprite"`, `needs_alpha:true`, NO chroma: `make tex` keeps the cut, and `bleed_alpha` scrubs the coloured glow gpt paints into the transparent texels. `edit_image` takes `background` too (verified re-cutting `decals/detail-ac.png`), so an opaque source can be re-emitted with alpha instead of hand-cut.
- **Fallback — the FLAT SOLID grey `#5a5d63` route**, for whatever transparent mode fumbles, and still MANDATORY for TRELLIS reference images (3D models section below). Generate on flat grey with the subject clear of the frame edges, register `class:"chroma"` (`chroma:"0x5a5d63"`, `tol:24`), and `make tex` bakes the key → alpha. On this route **NEVER ask the prompt for a "transparent background"** — with `background` unset gpt PAINTS A CHECKERBOARD as real pixels, a two-tone grid fused into the art that is strictly harder to cut than one flat colour.

  Windows/facades that must be transparent otherwise stay opaque and the tool warns until hand-edited.
- **Pipeline:** sources (1024²) in `textures-src/`, a symlink to the unversioned asset store; **the manifest `art/textures.json` is IN THE REPO and TRACKED** — it carries every prompt, crop and lift, so a commit's measured constants (an aspect lock, a rail fraction) stay explainable even though the pixels are not versioned. It is the source of truth (per label `{ src, class: tile|window|chroma|sprite, res?, crop?, needs_alpha, chroma?, tol?, prompt }`; `default_res` 512, details/sprites 256). Runtime outputs are build artifacts in `app/textures/<label>.png` (paths in `src/render/RenderConfig.hx` `TEXTURES`, loaded via `src/render/Textures.hx`) — never hand-place them. Subfolders are supported: a label/`src` like `decals/graffiti-1` nests both source and output (decals live under `textures-src/decals/` + `app/textures/decals/`). After generating/editing a source, update its `textures.json` entry (esp. `prompt`) and run **`make tex`** (→ `tools/textures.py`; also strips gpt prefixes and sweeps `*~` backups), then `make -C src` + reload. Two traps in that loop: **`res` is NOT in the tool's freshness test** (it tracks `lift` and `glass_alpha`, not the output size), so changing it alone reports *up to date* and rebuilds nothing — null the label's `last_converted` to force it; and **do not skip the reload by hot-swapping `material.map.image` over CDP when the res changed**, which renders garbage — see the bullet in [`debug-surface.md`](debug-surface.md).
- **Never manually `mv`/`cp` generated drops** (it trips a permission prompt). Generate straight into `textures-src/` (or a subdir) with `filename_prefix=<final-label>`, then run **`make adopt`** (`textures.py --rename`) — it strips the `image-<ts>-<hash>-` prefix in place (walks subdirs). Then `make tex`.
- **Batch gen in parallel:** issue independent `generate_image`/`edit_image` calls in ONE message (they're independent + slow). For lit/unlit pairs, make the unlit base first, then fan out lit edits.
- **Before reaching for `lift`, read the drop's CHANNEL SPREAD, not just its luminance.** A lift is a gamma and it publishes whatever cast the art already has — not by widening the spread but by raising the value the spread is read at. Measured on `facility/floor-lino`: lift 0.749 hit the target luminance exactly and the byte spread did not move (77/78/67 → 104/104/93, 10.8 either side), yet the corridor came out visibly green, because a cast invisible at 0.075 linear is visible at 0.137. **Over about 6 points of spread, re-roll instead of lifting** — with a named brightness plus "HUE: PERFECTLY NEUTRAL — the red, green and blue values are equal" and an explicit list of the casts to avoid. That drop returned spread **3.8**. Neutral surfaces here all render at G − B ≈ −2.6 (the rig is blue); anything sitting off that number is carrying a cast of its own.
- **NEVER stretch a texture** (any surface — walls, ground, roads, decals). World-aligned tiling on BOTH axes (`uv = worldPos/TILE` or `texture.repeat = faceLen/TILE`); accept a partial tile at a far edge. If stretching is ever unavoidable, STOP and notify the user. And NEVER put a flat solid-color material on visible geometry — always a texture.
